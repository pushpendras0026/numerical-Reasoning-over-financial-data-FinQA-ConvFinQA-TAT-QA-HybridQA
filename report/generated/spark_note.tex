On every development split together the store holds 1,360,644 facts from 9,375 questions over 4,395 deduplicated documents, with 1,884,748 dependency edges and 0 unresolved. It finds 491 pairs of documents with identical content across datasets, which is why related datasets are never pooled. Parquet takes 0.18~GB against 0.95~GB of JSON lines. Table~\ref{tab:spark} gives the honest performance picture: the same joins and checks take 15.8~s in single-process Python and at least 37.0~s in Spark (4.3.0). Spark gains from one core (54.5~s) to four (37.0~s) and slows again at eight (65.6~s) on this eight-core laptop. We did not profile further; fixed start-up cost and collecting results to the driver for the equality check are likely causes of the gap to Python. Parsing raw files into facts, done once in Python, takes 120~s and is the real bottleneck. At 1.4 million rows Spark's value here is a verified, partitioned columnar store rather than speed.
